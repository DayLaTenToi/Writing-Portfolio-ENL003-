In Toni Morrison's "Recitatif," the story is told from the first-person point of view of a girl named Twyla. This choice helps the reader see how unclear race can be and how people's memories are often wrong. Twyla and her roommate, Roberta, are described as looking "like salt and pepper," as we know they are different races, but Morrison hides which girl is Black and which is white. Also, because we only know what Twyla sees, readers have to face their own biases. We try to guess their races using clues, like Twyla's mother, Mary, wearing "ugly green slacks," or Roberta's mother wearing the "biggest cross."

At first, Twyla and Roberta become friends because they both feel left out. Unlike the "real orphans with beautiful dead parents," their mothers just "dumped" them at the shelter. When they grow up and become adults, their wealth and social classes change. This causes racial tension between them. At one point, it gets really heated during the school busing protests, where they hold opposing picket signs, which show how far apart they have drifted.

The biggest issue with Twyla's memory is about Maggie, the mute kitchen worker. Roberta says Maggie was a "black lady" and that the girls pushed her down. This goes against what Twyla remembers. Twyla quickly says, "She wasn't black," but later she admits to herself that she "actually couldn't be certain."

At the end of the day, the story shows that both women saw their own helpless mothers in Maggie. Twyla realizes, "Maggie was my dancing mother." By showing everything through Twyla's flawed memory, Morrison proves that how we feel about something can often be stronger than the actual facts.

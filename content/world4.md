As someone born in 2007, I grew up with Apple products under Tim Cook. I recently watched a YouTube video about how he turned Apple into a 4 trillion dollar company.

The video basically tells his story from an admiring point of view, mixing history with business talk. It shows Cook not as a flashy creator like Steve Jobs but as a very disciplined and practical manager.

This way of looking at things honestly focuses on efficiency, controlling the supply chain, and quiet leadership instead of just making things look cool. The video highlights how he managed inventory by cutting stock time from 30 days to just six. It also shows his strong defense of user privacy against the FBI to prove that good management and caring about rights are the real reasons for its success today.

Actually, growing up, iPads, iPhones, AirPods,... just existed in my daily life, so I always look at things from a customer's perspective. Therefore, looking at it from this business side was totally eye-opening for me. I never really thought about the complicated political stuff Apple had to deal with in China or the hidden manufacturing steps that make these devices possible. It changes my opinion from just liking the smooth screens on my devices to respecting the massive system that keeps everything running.

However, if this story were told from another point of view, like a factory worker at Foxconn or a tech critic who misses the Steve Jobs days, the tone would completely change. A product critic might like to say Cook's time is boring and focus on the failed Apple Car instead of the success of Apple Services. At the same time, a factory worker's point of view would probably show the harsh human cost needed to meet those huge, perfectly timed production goals. This would replace the story of business success with a harsh look at how workers are treated.

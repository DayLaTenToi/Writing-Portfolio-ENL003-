### 2. Reflection Questions

**What patterns of feedback did you notice?**
The positive feedback consistently highlighted that my in-text analysis was strong and connected well to the overarching themes of the essay. However, the critical feedback revealed a pattern of missing context in my introduction. My reviewer noted that my personal opening lacked the necessary background about my first language, and more importantly, that I failed to clearly state which poetry collection I was actually analyzing.

**What specific aspect of your draft is most important to address in your revision?**
The most important aspect to address is my introduction. Because I only introduced the specific poems ("Still I Rise" and "Caged Bird") rather than the collection as a whole, my reader was left confused.

**What did you notice about your peer's papers that you might want to incorporate into your own draft?**
Looking at Ava Mitchell's essay on Gwendolyn Brooks, I noticed how effectively she handled her introduction. She immediately provided historical background on the poet, explicitly named the collection (*Annie Allen*), and delivered a very clear thesis regarding the collection's themes of urban poverty and female agency right in the first paragraph. Furthermore, Ava included proper formatting that I am currently missing, such as citing specific line numbers for her quotes (e.g., "(Brooks, 38)") and including a Works Cited page at the end of her document. I need to incorporate both of these elements to make my draft more professional.

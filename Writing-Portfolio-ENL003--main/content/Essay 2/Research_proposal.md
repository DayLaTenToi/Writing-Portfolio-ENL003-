# Research Proposal

**Working Title:** Humanology, Social Ecology, and the Paradox of Domination in Octavia E. Butler's *Dawn*

**Primary Text:** *Dawn* by Octavia E. Butler (1987)

**Context:** Octavia E. Butler's *Dawn* follows Lilith Iyapo, a human survivor rescued by an alien species called the Oankali after a devastating nuclear war on Earth. While the Oankali present themselves as benevolent saviors who have healed the planet, their rescue demands a forced genetic merger that strips humanity of its biological self-determination and consent. I am specifically interested in how the novel frames the Oankali's diagnosis of humanity's "fatal flaw", the combination of intelligence and hierarchy, and how this diagnosis functions as an alternative form of colonial domination. Through Lilith's role as an uneasy mediator who experiences both captivity and compromise, the text interrogates the fine line between survival and subjugation.

**Preliminary Research Question:** How does Octavia E. Butler's *Dawn* utilize the complex characterization and compromised agency of Lilith Iyapo to challenge the Oankali's biologically deterministic view of human hierarchy, and how does this narrative framework align with Murray Bookchin's theories of social ecology?

**Why Your Question Matters:** This question is intellectually and culturally significant because it complicates standard science-fiction tropes of alien invasions and benevolent rescues, exposing the hidden logic of domination within the concept of a savior. Culturally, it forces us to confront how modern environmental crises are deeply intertwined with social hierarchies and systems of power rather than just being scientific or technological failures. Personally and philosophically, analyzing Lilith's resistance and empathy within a coercive system highlights the enduring human capacity for ethical relationships, proving that our future depends on transforming how we relate to one another rather than just altering our biology.

**One Potential Secondary Source:** Bookchin, Murray. *The Ecology of Freedom: The Emergence and Dissolution of Hierarchy*. Cheshire Books, 1982. (This text is foundational for establishing how the human domination of nature stems directly from social hierarchies among humans).
